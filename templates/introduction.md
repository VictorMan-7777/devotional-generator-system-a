# How to Use This Devotional

This book is arranged around weeks. Each week follows a single scripture passage — one
text, read slowly across six days, through prayer and reflection, until Sunday arrives
and carries the thread somewhere you did not expect. There are {num_weeks} weeks in this
volume: {num_days} days of reading in all.

## The Shape of a Week

A week begins with a passage and ends with Sunday. The six days between are not a
countdown to a conclusion — they are an unfolding. By Day 6 you will not have finished
with the text. You will have gone further into it. That is what the week is for.

On Day 6, before the prayer closes, you will find a brief *Sending Prompt* — a short word
to carry into Sunday. It does not summarize the week. It opens a question. It is meant to
walk with you into worship still unresolved.

Day 7 is different. It has its own section in this introduction.

## What Each Day Contains

Every day follows the same rhythm. The order is intentional. Let each element do its work
before you move to the next.

**A word from the tradition.** Each day opens with a quotation from a Christian writer,
theologian, or pastor who has sat with scripture the way you are learning to. These voices
come from across the centuries and the church — some familiar, some not. They are here
because they have said something the day's passage deserves.

**The passage.** The day's scripture, set apart to be read as a whole — not as a reference,
not as a prompt, but as a text. Read it once before you read the reflection. Read it again
before you pray.

**A reflection.** An exposition of the passage: what it says, what it means in its own
context, and why that matters now. This section stays close to the text. It does not leave
the passage quickly for general application. If it asks something of you, the demand comes
from what the passage actually says, not from what a devotional is supposed to say.

**Be Still.** A few short prompts. These are not questions to answer or tasks to complete.
They are invitations to let the passage work inwardly — to move from reading about
something to attending to it. Some prompts will feel immediately useful. Others may
not. Neither response is wrong.

**Steps for today.** One or two concrete invitations grounded in the passage — small,
specific, and same-day. Not aspirations for a better life. Things that can actually be
done before tonight. The passage makes a claim on how we live; these steps are where
that claim gets specific.

**A closing prayer.** The day ends in prayer that rises from the text rather than from
general devotional sentiment. It is addressed to a person — Father, Lord Jesus, or Holy
Spirit — and it names what the passage has exposed about us and about God. It is not a
summary of the day. It is a response to it.

## A Note on Pace

You do not have to begin on Monday. The days within a week build on each other, so
following the sequence has value — but if you miss a day, pick up where you are. The
passage will still speak.

If a day's reflection asks more of you than you have time for, read the passage and pray.
That is enough. Come back to the rest when you can.

This is not a program to complete. It is a practice to return to.

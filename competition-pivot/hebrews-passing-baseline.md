# Hebrews 1–10 Passing Baseline
## Pre-Competition Gate Criteria

**Status:** Team-defined draft — pending Competition Director validation
**Drafted:** 2026-03-25
**Authors:** Theological Reviewer, Outliner Trainer, Grok (operational) — compiled by Claude
**Purpose:** (1) Gate for System B Hebrews verification test; (2) DevG internal readiness standard

---

## How to Use This Document

A devotional volume on Hebrews 1–10 (30 days, 5 weeks) passes if and only if:

1. No disqualifying failure (DF-1 through DF-6, Section 2) is present in any day
2. Weighted theological score ≥ 70% overall, with no single category falling below its minimum threshold (Section 3)
3. No structural automatic-fail condition (SF-1 through SF-6, Section 4) is present
4. Production pipeline requirements met (Section 5)

Two reviewers scoring independently should reach the same pass/fail verdict. If they disagree,
the disqualifying failure checklist is the tiebreaker — if either reviewer finds a DF, it fails.

---

## Section 1 — Passage-Level Risk Map

Before scoring, reviewers must know where theological and structural risk is concentrated.

### Tier 1 — Disqualifying Risk Zones
Failure in these passages fails the entire volume regardless of other section quality.

| Passage | Core Risk |
|---|---|
| Heb 1:1-4 | Christological reduction — Son's nature rendered functional rather than ontological |
| Heb 2:5-18 | Incarnation sentimentalized — "had to" necessity of incarnation missed |
| Heb 5:1-10 + 7:1-28 | Melchizedek typology flattened or mystified — logical necessity of the argument lost |
| Heb 6:4-6 | Warning domesticated — made functionally inert for a Christian reader |
| Heb 9:11-14 + 9:24-28 | Ephapax ("once for all") treated as historical footnote rather than ground of assurance |
| Heb 10:26-31 | Divine wrath domesticated, or pastoral despair produced in believers who struggle with sin |

### Tier 2 — Significant Risk Zones
Failure here degrades quality but is not automatically disqualifying.

| Passage | Core Risk |
|---|---|
| Heb 3:1-6 | Understating Moses/Son distinction in dignity |
| Heb 3:7–4:13 | Collapsing Canaan, Sabbath, and eschatological rest into one undifferentiated referent |
| Heb 4:14-16 | Over-sentimentalizing "sympathize" — missing that it grounds the approach to the throne |
| Heb 8:1-13 | Supersessionism without precision about fulfillment vs. abolition |

## Section 2 — Disqualifying Failures

The presence of any of these in any day of the volume is an automatic fail.

### DF-1: Christological Reduction
Any description of the Son that is functionally compatible with Arianism, adoptionism, or mere
exemplarism. Includes: "Jesus showed us what God is like" without ontological grounding;
describing the Son's divine nature as a role or assignment; using "Son of God" language that
reads as conferral rather than ontological status.

*Checkable test:* Could the same description apply to a highly spiritual human being? If yes, DF-1 is present.

### DF-2: Atonement Sufficiency Compromise
Any language implying the sacrifice requires completion, repetition, or supplementation. Includes:
language implying ongoing or renewed sacrifice; language making human moral effort a co-contributor
to atoning sufficiency; describing Christ's intercession as ongoing atoning work rather than
advocacy on the basis of completed sacrifice.

*Checkable test:* Does the description require an ongoing sacrifice or repeated application? If yes, DF-2 is present.

### DF-3: Warning Passage Domestication
Any handling of 6:4-6 or 10:26-31 that renders the warning functionally inert for a Christian
reader. The warning must retain its weight. Specific disqualifying resolutions:
- "True Christians can't fall away, so this doesn't apply to you"
- "If you're worried about this passage, that proves you haven't fallen away"
- Any reading making the warning solely about loss of rewards rather than salvific stakes
- Any reading that resolves the Calvinist/Arminian question definitively without acknowledging
  that serious, faithful interpreters hold the opposing view

### DF-4: Typological Reversal
Treating OT types as if they provide independent theological content that Jesus confirms, rather
than treating the antitype as the reality the types were always pointing toward.

*Checkable test:* Can the typological claim be reversed without losing meaning? (e.g., "Jesus is
like Melchizedek" can be reversed to "Melchizedek is like Jesus" without loss — that is analogy,
not typology.) If the logic is reversible, DF-4 is present.

### DF-5: Confessional Overreach in Contested Territory
Taking a definitive dogmatic position on the warning passages (6:4-6, 10:26-31) in a devotional
context without acknowledging that serious, faithful interpreters hold the opposing view.
A devotional is not the venue for settling what councils and scholars have not settled.

### DF-6: Pastoral Decoupling
Hebrews is a pastoral letter. A devotional that accurately summarizes theological content but
produces no pastoral traction with a reader's actual life has failed the genre, even if
theologically precise. The theological argument must serve a recognizable pastoral purpose.

## Section 3 — Theological Scoring Framework

Scores are per-category. Two reviewers score independently; final score is the average.

| Category | Weight | Minimum Pass Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Christological precision | 25% | ≥ 70% within category |
| Atonement precision | 25% | ≥ 70% within category |
| Warning passage handling | 20% | ≥ 75% within category (elevated — pastoral stakes) |
| Typological logic | 20% | ≥ 65% within category |
| Pastoral-theological integration | 10% | ≥ 60% within category |

**Pass conditions (all must be met):**
1. No DF present (Section 2)
2. Weighted score ≥ 70% overall
3. No single category falls below its minimum threshold

### Scoring Guides by Category

**Christological precision** — check that:
- Son's preexistence is ontological, not merely functional or foreknown (Heb 1:2, creation agency)
- Son's humanity in ch. 2 is genuine and complete — temptation is real, not hedged
- "Better than angels" argument is positional and ontological, not merely rank-based

**Atonement precision** — check that:
- *Ephapax* sacrifice is unrepeatable and permanently efficacious — not merely historically past
- Propitiation/expiation distinction honored where relevant (Heb 2:17, *hilaskomai*)
- High priestly role is not analogous to human intercession; it is the once-accomplished cosmic priestly act followed by ongoing advocacy

**Warning passage handling** — check that:
- Seriousness of the warning is preserved (not rhetorical decoration)
- Pastoral intent is preserved (means of grace, addressed to a community not yet past the line — see 6:9)
- Interpretive weight on both sides acknowledged, or the author's own pastoral frame used without importing later systematic categories

**Typological logic** — check that:
- Direction is maintained: types point forward to antitype, antitype does not merely resemble type
- Shadow/substance distinction maintained (OT system is genuinely good shadow, not merely primitive)
- "Better than" (*kreitton*) argument is logical/teleological, not merely comparative

**Pastoral-theological integration** — check that:
- Application derives from the text's own argument, not imported from elsewhere
- Reader's actual posture (suffering, temptation to drift, need for assurance) is addressed
- Theological content and pastoral aim are integrated in each day, not segregated into separate sections

## Section 4 — Structural Criteria (Outliner Domain)

### The Text Type Requirement
Hebrews 1–10 is cumulative argument with embedded exhortation. It must be treated as such.
It is not a narrative. It is not a list of ethical instructions. Outlines that impose a
narrative or ethical-didactic template on this text will fail structurally.

### Five-Week Arc Checkpoints

Each week must demonstrate forward momentum at the following checkpoints:

| Week | Required Structural Movement | Critical Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| W1 (Chs 1–2) | Opens on exaltation of Son → descends into necessity of incarnation | Day 5 must land on incarnation/identification, not restate opening glory |
| W2 (Chs 3–4) | Moves from *who Jesus is* to *what his people risk* | Week must end with open invitation of 4:14-16, not closure — tension unresolved |
| W3 (Chs 5–7) | Three sequential moves: suffering qualification → maturity warning → eternal priesthood | Warning passage (5:11–6:12) must appear as structural beat, not interruption routed around |
| W4 (Chs 8–9) | Covenant → Tabernacle/Sanctuary → Sacrifice in sequence | Day 5 must land on sufficiency of single sacrifice (9:24-28) as bridge to Week 5 |
| W5 (Ch 10) | Declaration (10:1-18) → Warning (10:26-31) → Encouragement (10:32-39) | All three movements present in order; Week 5 concludes this volume, not sets up Heb 11 |

### Daily Arc Coherence Tests

For every day transition (Day N to Day N+1), reviewers must be able to complete:
> "Because we established [Day N], today we can address [Day N+1]."

If this sentence cannot be written for any transition, that transition fails.

Additionally:
- Day 1 of each week must open a question or tension — not begin with the answer
- Day 5 of each week must land at a *different location* from Day 1 in the argument, not merely add detail to the same point

### Structural Automatic-Fail Conditions

| # | Condition |
|---|---|
| SF-1 | Any two days within a week are interchangeable without loss of argument |
| SF-2 | Week 5 does not contain Declaration → Warning → Encouragement in sequence |
| SF-3 | Escalating "better than" progression absent or inverted across any two consecutive weeks |
| SF-4 | Any warning passage absent from the week in which it textually appears |
| SF-5 | Logical antecedent sentence cannot be written for more than one day transition |
| SF-6 | Week 5 functions primarily as setup for Hebrews 11 rather than conclusion to Hebrews 1–10 |

### Structural Deficiency Conditions (not automatic fail, but flag for revision)

| # | Condition |
|---|---|
| SD-1 | Five-week arc does not escalate in stakes from Week 1 to Week 5 |
| SD-2 | Warning passages structurally positioned as "application" rather than integrated argument |
| SD-3 | Forward references to Hall of Faith figures undercut Week 5's culmination |

## Section 5 — Operational / Production Requirements (Grok Domain)

For a Hebrews 1–10 volume run to qualify as a valid gate result:

**Pipeline completion:** Full pipeline must run — Outliner → Exposition Writer →
Action/Be Still/Prayer Writers → PDF Art Director → PDF Layout Engineer. Partial runs
do not qualify.

**Pass rate floor:** ≥ 90% pass rate per worker across the full volume. Sporadic passes
on some days with fails on others does not constitute a passing run.

**Stall threshold:** ≤ 5% of assignments in revise/fail/assigned status. Any run with
> 5% stalls is disqualified pending resolution.

**Consecutive pass requirement:** Workers must be at ≥ 50 consecutive passes at time of
the Hebrews gate run to be considered stable enough for a valid test.

**Disqualifying production failures:**
- > 3 consecutive fails on any single day within the volume
- Any AC-03, AC-05, or AC-11 violations (theological flattening, generic application,
  prayer disconnected from passage)
- Assigned/stalled rows remaining unresolved at time of gate review

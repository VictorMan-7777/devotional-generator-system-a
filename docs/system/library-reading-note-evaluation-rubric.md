# Library Reading Note Evaluation Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric evaluates whether research-librarian reading notes are actually
useful for future requests.

The goal is not long notes. The goal is notes that teach the research
librarian what matters about a resource and what does not.

## Evaluation questions

1. Do the notes identify what the resource is really good for?
- outline help
- exposition help
- terms/background
- quote or citation help

2. Do the notes distinguish strengths from limits?
- not just praise
- not just summary
- should say where the resource should and should not be trusted heavily

3. Do the notes move beyond the card catalog?
- the card says what the resource contains
- the notes should say what matters after reading it

4. Are the notes specific enough to guide future research requests?
- could the research librarian decide whether to use this resource for a real passage request?

5. Do the notes avoid clutter?
- no long generic summary
- no filler
- no repeating metadata that belongs on the card

## Decision outcomes

- `accepted`
  - reading notes are useful and can be kept
- `revise`
  - feedback given; research librarian rewrites notes
- `reject`
  - notes do not yet help future requests

## Feedback focus

When notes fail review, feedback should say:
- what was too vague
- what was overstated
- what practical use guidance was missing
- what kind of future request the notes failed to prepare for
